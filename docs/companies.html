How companies are judged

  • Ten business checks use up to 10 years of annual results from SEC filings: consistent profits, return on equity, gross and net margins, free cash flow, earnings backed by cash, debt, capital needs, per-share growth and share dilution.
  • Intrinsic value discounts the average free cash flow of the last 3 years ("owner earnings") at a year, assuming growth equal to the lower of past revenue and EPS growth (capped at ) for 10 years, then .
  • Buy-below price applies a margin of safety to that value. A company is a buy candidate only if it passes most checks (including profits and debt), trades below that price and yields more than the 10-year Treasury.
  • IPOs are scored the same way. Most will show "too early": Buffett rarely buys IPOs because the seller picks the timing and the price, and a new company lacks a long record. Insider lock-ups typically end about 180 days after listing.
  • Discover scans 250 NYSE/Nasdaq companies a day (about 6,000 in total, each rescanned monthly) and keeps the smaller, fast-growing ones that pass the business checks. You see 25 random picks each visit; the pool grows over the first few weeks.
  • Numbers can't see management quality or how durable a moat really is, which Buffett and Munger weigh heavily. Treat this as a first filter for further research, not investment advice.

Data: SEC EDGAR financial filings, share prices from Yahoo Finance (Stooq fallback), Treasury yield from FRED. Prices can be delayed. Financials are annual and may lag the latest quarter.
